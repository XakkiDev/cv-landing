# 3D scroll — experimental work in progress

`src/scroll-3d.js` uses Three.js and a continuous indexed sheet. `src/scroll-geometry.js` maps the sheet's material coordinate onto two tangential Archimedean rolls. The roll radius decreases from the outside toward the inner end, with a spacing of one paper thickness per turn. This is a kinematic approximation of thin paper, not a cloth simulation: the path depends on the scroll offset and does not model bending stiffness, collisions or friction.

The front of the mesh carries an HTML snapshot; the back uses an unprinted material. `src/paper-material.js` applies the same procedural WebGL ageing and fibres to both sides. Short blank leading and trailing margins form shallow end curls when the document is fully open. A visible range of the continuous mesh is limited while open, so only the bent edges rather than full rolls are displayed. The two rolls begin from one closed roll: the upper edge starts rolling only after the sheet begins opening. The closed scroll is angled to expose the end. Rapier precomputes the closing drop; the 2D photograph cannot receive physically correct 3D shadows. Seal and cord are deliberately disabled while their designs are reconsidered. Legacy saved seal data is retained but unused.

The site always starts closed after reload, preserving legacy seal data in browser `localStorage` for future use. The open 3D sheet can be rotated with right-drag or the small arrow controls; its temporary X/Y/Z angles are displayed in degrees. The previous HTML reader is used if WebGL cannot be created. Text is a single html2canvas bitmap capped for common texture limits. Tiled textures, a proper accessible reader and responsive texture regeneration are future improvements.

Run `node --test tests/*.test.js` for numerical tests and `node scripts/check-webgl.mjs` to run the browser scenario. GitHub Actions runs both and records screenshots, video and diagnostics. Review the actual frames as well: passing numerical tests does not prove realistic appearance or interaction. SwiftShader does not predict performance on a user's GPU.

The separately installed shader-effects-inc/shaders bundle uses WebGPU; it is not integrated into the Three.js WebGL material.
